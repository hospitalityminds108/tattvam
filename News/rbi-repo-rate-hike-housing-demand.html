Mumbai, October 2026: The Reserve Bank of India raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% on 7 October 2026. A Realty N Infra report published the next day gathered reactions from developers across the country, including Raahul Maroo, Founder & Managing Director of Tat:vm Builders & Developers Pvt Ltd. His view is that buyers will become more careful, but demand for homes will hold.

What Raahul Maroo said

Mr. Maroo told the publication that the hike could mean higher EMIs or longer loan tenures for affected borrowers and that some first-time buyers may become more cautious as a result.

He expects buyers to change how they evaluate property. They are likely to be more sensitive to price, compare more projects before deciding, and show “a stronger preference for projects offering compelling value.”


He does not expect a 25-basis-point increase to fundamentally weaken housing demand. Buyers with stable incomes and a clear intention to purchase, he said, are likely to go ahead.

How other developers see it

The report shows a range of opinion across the industry:

  • Affordability pressure: Several developers expect higher borrowing costs to slow decisions in the affordable and mid-income segments, especially for first-time and loan-dependent buyers.

  • Limited impact: Others said home loan rates remain attractive and that festive offers will keep end-users buying.

  • Faster decisions: One developer suggested some buyers may book sooner to lock in prices before costs rise further.

  • Cost of capital: Developers also face costlier funding. The report notes this issue matters most in Mumbai redevelopment, where projects depend on financing, approvals, and society consensus.


Most developers quoted said a stable rate environment would have given buyers and builders more confidence.

What this means if you are buying a home

A quarter-point increase changes the monthly EMI on a typical home loan, and the effect adds up over a long tenure. How much and how soon depends on your lender, since each bank decides when to pass the increase on.

The wider picture

The hike comes at a busy time for the market. Mr Maroo recently wrote about why the festive season is India’s homebuying season, and he spoke to Hindustan Times about rising unsold inventory and pricing limits. His comments here are consistent with both: buyers will reward value, and builders in Mumbai will need discipline.
