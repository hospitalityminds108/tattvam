Mumbai, October 2026: Ask any Indian family when they plan to book a home, and the answer usually falls between Ganesh Chaturthi and Diwali. In a column published by 99acres on 7 October 2026, Raahul Maroo, Founder & Managing Director of Tat:vm Builders & Developers Pvt Ltd., explains why. His argument is that festive offers are only a small part of the story.

A four-month window, not a weekend sale

Mr. Maroo describes the stretch from Ganesh Chaturthi through Navratri, Dussehra, and Diwali as a period of nearly four months when families, developers, banks, and governments all move in the same direction. He calls the result a unique “home buying season."

Tradition sets the calendar.

The starting point is the muhurat. Indian households have long chosen auspicious dates for big commitments, and a home is the biggest of them. Mr. Maroo points out that booking, possession, and Griha Pravesh are all planned around the festive calendar, so a cultural habit ends up driving real economic activity.

The same logic explains the lull just before it, which we covered in our note on why buyers pause property decisions during Pitru Paksha.

The whole family is finally in one room. children,

A home in India is rarely one person’s decision. Parents, spouses, children, and often the extended family weigh in on location, schools, hospitals, and amenities, especially when the home is for self-use.


Mr Maroo observes that festivals solve a practical problem: everyone is in town. Family reunions and visits from relatives abroad make site visits and money conversations much easier, and months of research can turn into a decision. For families based overseas, our NRI Services desk supports the same process.

Banks, developers, and policy line up

The column also looks at the three institutions that respond to this demand:

  • Banks: Most buyers rely on long-term loans, so the cost and availability of credit matter a great deal. Lenders step up their schemes during this period, which lets buyers compare interest rates, processing charges, loan structures, and repayment terms.

  • Developers: New launches and fresh inventory are timed for the season. Buyers who have compared options for months get more choice and a reason to close.

  • Government: Measures such as stamp duty relief or infrastructure announcements, when they coincide with festive demand, improve sentiment and affordability.

What this means for a Mumbai homebuyer

Mr. Maroo’s conclusion is that the season brings together good timing, family consensus, and financial readiness, three things that seldom arrive at once.


At Tat:vm, we would add one caution: an auspicious date should speed up a well-researched decision, not replace the research. Frequently Asked Questions

1. Why is the festive season considered the best time to buy a home in India?+

Because several things line up at once. Auspicious dates fall in this period; families are together to decide, banks run home loan schemes, and developers release new projects. Raahul Maroo, Founder & MD of Tat:vm, makes this point in his 99 acres column: the season works because timing, family agreement, and finances come together.

2. Which months make up India’s homebuying season?+

It runs for nearly four months, starting with Ganesh Chaturthi and continuing through Navratri, Dussehra, and Diwali into the auspicious dates that follow.

3. What is a muhurat, and why does it matter in property buying?+

A muhurat is an auspicious time chosen for important beginnings. Many Indian families plan the booking, possession, and Griha Pravesh of a home around these dates, which is why sales activity rises in the festive months.

4. Why do buyers wait until Navratri to book a home?+

The fortnight before Navratri is Pitru Paksha, which many families consider unsuitable for new purchases. Decisions are deferred and then pick up once Navratri begins. We explain this in our note on Pitru Paksha and property purchases.
