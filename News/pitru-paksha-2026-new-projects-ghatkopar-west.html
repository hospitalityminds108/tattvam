Many Mumbai homebuyers put property bookings and registrations on hold during Pitru Paksha, which runs from 26 September to 10 October 2026, because the period is traditionally seen as inauspicious for new beginnings. The pause does not have to be wasted time. If you are considering new projects in Ghatkopar West, these two weeks are the ideal window to research, compare and prepare, so you can book with confidence once Navratri begins.

This guide explains why the pause happens, what developers expect after it, and a practical two-week plan covering project checks, developer due diligence and your full purchase budget. It draws on Tat:vm's experience building in Ghatkopar and across Mumbai.

Key Takeaways

  • The pause is sentiment, not a slowdown. Developers expect deferred demand to return during Navratri, Dussehra and Diwali.

  • Verify before you pay a token. Check every project on the MahaRERA portal for registration, approvals and progress reports.

Why Do Homebuyers Pause Property Purchases During Pitru Paksha?

Pitru Paksha, also called Shraddh, is a fortnight dedicated to honouring ancestors, and many Hindu families consider it inauspicious for new beginnings such as buying a home, booking a flat or registering a sale. So, unless a deal is urgent, buyers and sellers often defer paperwork until Navratri, Dussehra or Diwali.

In Mumbai, the effect is visible across deal sizes. Hindustan Times reported on a city landowner whose negotiations to sell suburban land to a developer were paused for the fortnight. The family planned to restart calls and paperwork on the first day of Navratri, aim to close by Dussehra, and sign the memorandum of understanding around Dhanteras or Diwali.

What developers expect after the pause

Speaking to Hindustan Times, Tat:vm founder Raahul Maroo said: "Buying a home in India is rarely just a financial decision; it is deeply rooted in emotion, belief and sentiment." He described the fortnight as a temporary, sentiment-led pause rather than a sign of weaker demand. With the festive season usually bringing renewed optimism, he expects deferred intent to support healthy housing activity once Navratri begins.

That emotional side of home buying is something we understand well; it runs through our brand story Respecting the belief and planning well are not in conflict. The families who book with the least stress in Navratri are usually the ones who did their homework during Shraddh.

How to Shortlist New Projects in Ghatkopar West in Two Weeks

Split the fortnight into two halves: spend the first week deciding what you need and building a shortlist, and the second week verifying projects and arranging finance. By the time Navratri begins, you should have two or three projects you trust and a clear budget for each.

Why Ghatkopar West is on so many shortlists

Ghatkopar West combines a Central line station with the eastern end of Metro Line 1, which links it to Andheri and the western suburbs. LBS Marg and the Eastern Express Highway are close by, and the area has established schools, hospitals and markets. That mix of connectivity and everyday convenience keeps demand steady from both families and tenants.

What Will Your Home Really Cost Beyond the Price?

In Mumbai, add stamp duty of 6% (5% if a woman is the sole buyer of a residential property), a registration fee of 1% capped at Rs 30,000, and GST if the home is under construction. Stamp duty is charged on the higher of your agreement value or the government's Ready Reckoner rate for that location, as notified by IGR Maharashtra

For an under-construction home outside the affordable housing category, GST of 5% also applies; ready homes with an Occupancy Certificate carry no GST. Current rates are published on the CBIC GST portal. Add legal fees, society charges, parking, interiors and moving costs, and confirm every figure with your lawyer before booking.

How Should You Check a Developer Before Booking?

Judge the developer as carefully as the flat. Among real estate developers in Mumbai, the ones worth trusting have delivered projects you can visit, clean regulatory records and clear answers in writing. Use the pause to check all three.

  • Search MahaRERA. Look up the project and the promoter on the MahaRERA portal Confirm the registration is active, note the committed completion date, and read the latest quarterly progress report.
  • Visit completed projects. Walk through a building the developer finished a few years ago. Look at the lobby, lifts, walls and common areas, and talk to residents if you can.
  • Read the documents. Ask for the approved plans, the carpet area of your unit, the payment schedule and the draft agreement for sale. Have a lawyer review them.
  • Ask how the building is planned. Developers who coordinate design and construction carefully tend to deliver fewer defects and more predictable timelines.
  • Get promises in writing. Parking, amenities, possession date and any festive offer should appear in the agreement, not only in a brochure.

The best real estate developers in Mumbai welcome these questions. Any hesitation to answer them is useful information in itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Pitru Paksha in 2026?

Pitru Paksha 2026 is observed from 26 September to 10 October 2026. Navratri follows immediately after, and many Mumbai homebuyers plan bookings and registrations for Navratri, Dussehra, Dhanteras or Diwali.

Is Pitru Paksha a bad time to buy property?

Many Hindu families consider Pitru Paksha inauspicious for new beginnings, so they avoid booking or registering property during it. It is a matter of personal belief. Developers describe it as a temporary, sentiment-led pause rather than a sign of weak demand.

Can I visit sites and research projects during Shraddh?

Yes. Many buyers who avoid booking during Shraddh still visit sites, compare projects, check MahaRERA records and arrange home loan approvals, so they are ready to book once the festive season begins.

What is the stamp duty on a flat in Mumbai?

Stamp duty in Mumbai is 6% of the higher of the agreement value or the Ready Reckoner rate, and 5% when a woman is the sole buyer of a residential property. A registration fee of 1%, capped at Rs 30,000, is added.

How do I check if a project is MahaRERA registered?

Search the project name or registration number under Registered Projects on maharera.maharashtra.gov.in. Confirm the status is active, then review the approvals, completion date and quarterly progress reports.
