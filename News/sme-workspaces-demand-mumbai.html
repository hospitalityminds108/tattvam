Demand for quality office space in Mumbai is rising from a new direction. Small and medium enterprises (SMEs) are leaving industrial galas and nondescript addresses for compact Grade A workplaces, and the high valuations seen on the SME exchanges are a big part of the reason. The Hindu reported on this shift on 19 September 2026, with comments from Raahul Maroo, Founder and Managing Director of Tat:vm.

This article summarises what the report found, why promoters now treat the office as part of their valuation story, and how Tat:vm is responding as a Mumbai developer.

Key Takeaways

  • SMEs have become a distinct source of demand for Grade A office space in Mumbai.
  • The typical requirement is about 1,000 to 5,000 sq ft in a well-connected location.
  • Micro-CBDs such as Ghatkopar, Andheri, Kurla and Powai are the preferred locations.
  • As of September 2026, 136 SME IPOs had been listed, several with issue sizes between ₹25 crore and ₹150 crore.
  • Boutique developers, Tat:vm among them, are designing smaller commercial units for this segment.

What Did The Hindu Report?

The report describes a section of SMEs upgrading from shop-floor offices to high-quality real estate. Developers and analysts told the newspaper that the purpose is twofold: to meet clients and investors at a credible address, and to give employees a better working environment.

To serve this demand, developers are designing office buildings specifically for smaller occupiers. These buildings offer accessibility, connectivity, parking, a greener setting, larger common areas and modern amenities alongside the core office unit.

Why Are Valuations Changing the Way Promoters Think About Offices?

SME listings have put real capital in the hands of smaller companies. With 136 SME IPOs listed as of September 2026, and several raising between ₹25 crore and ₹150 crore, the segment has the liquidity to invest in better premises. Government support for small businesses has also raised their ambitions.

A listing changes who walks through the door. Investors, bankers and analysts visit, and they form a view of the business from what they see. Promoters who once treated the office purely as a cost now see it as part of the company's credibility. For a practical guide to choosing such a space, read our article on commercial office space in Mumbai for SMEs.

What Did Raahul Maroo Say?

Speaking to The Hindu, Tat:vm founder Raahul Maroo said: "We are seeing a rapid change in SMEs." He explained that their interest in Grade A space is about creating a working area that can attract institutional interest and add value for their own clients.

He also pointed out that SMEs want offices that match the quality and ambition of their businesses while staying within tight budgets, and that Tat:vm has built a niche product around that need. A group of boutique developers, he added, is now building smaller commercial offices for this segment.

Which Locations Are SMEs Choosing?

The report names Mumbai's upcoming micro-CBDs as the main beneficiaries:

  • Ghatkopar: Central line, Metro Line 1 and the Eastern Express Highway.
  • Andheri: Western line and metro connectivity, close to the airport.
  • Kurla: Central and Harbour lines, next to BKC.
  • Powai: a road-led business district popular with technology firms and startups.

These locations give smaller firms a recognised business address without the entry cost of the traditional commercial core.

What Is Happening in the Wider Market?

The trend is not limited to Mumbai. Anarock Commercial's Managing Director, Bappaditya Basu, told The Hindu that smaller businesses and startups are clearly moving from ad hoc or low-grade space to professionally managed, well-addressed workplaces. Startups now account for 37.5% of all flexible office seats rented nationally, more than corporates and multinationals combined, and the average deal size in seats has more than doubled.

According to the report, this demand has pushed developers and flex operators to design for smaller occupiers, and it is one reason India has become the largest flexible office market in the Asia-Pacific region.

Conclusion

The move from galas to Grade A offices shows how Indian SMEs are maturing: better funded, more visible to investors and more competitive for talent. For promoters, the right office is now part of how the business is valued.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are SMEs in Mumbai moving to Grade A offices?

Many SMEs now raise money through SME exchange listings or institutional investors. A professional address helps them meet clients and investors with confidence and gives employees a better place to work than an industrial gala or a converted flat.

How much office space do these SMEs usually look for?

According to developers quoted by The Hindu, the typical requirement is about 1,000 to 5,000 sq ft in a well-connected location, inside a building that offers parking, larger common areas and modern amenities.

Which Mumbai locations are SMEs considering?

Emerging micro-CBDs such as Ghatkopar, Andheri, Kurla and Powai are on most shortlists. They combine rail, metro and highway access with newer Grade A buildings at a lower entry cost than the traditional business core.

What does an SME listing have to do with office space?

A listed or listing-bound company is judged by investors, bankers and analysts who visit its office. Promoters therefore treat the workplace as part of the company's credibility and valuation story, not only as a cost.

Does Tat:vm build commercial offices for SMEs?

Yes. Tat:vm is planning compact commercial workplaces designed around light, air and space, including the upcoming Marine Lines commercial project and street-facing commercial space in the Ghatkopar mixed-use development.
