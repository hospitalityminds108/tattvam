Why redevelopment is Mumbai’s kindest option

Blog · 5 min readRead →

Aging buildings on prime land are the city’s biggest opportunity. Here’s how society redevelopment works — fairly.

Across Mumbai, thousands of housing societies sit on strong land and weakening concrete. The frame is tired; the location is not. Redevelopment is how a building’s promise is renewed without asking a single family to leave the neighbourhood it loves.

Done fairly, redevelopment means larger carpet areas, safer RCC structure, working lifts and fire compliance — and a community that stays whole. Done badly, it means years in rented limbo and broken trust.

The difference is process. A RERA-registered developer, written area entitlements, milestone-linked timelines and member-first meetings are not extras; they are the minimum. When a society keeps its address and gains fifty more years, that is the kindest thing Mumbai can do for itself.

The five fundamentals, explained simply

Blog · 4 min readRead →

Space, light, air, community, sustainability — what each means, and why they only work together.

Space is rooms that hold a morning, not just furniture. We design for how a family actually lives — the study corner, the drying yard, the chai by the window.

Light is a basic right. Orientation, windows and balconies are placed before saleable area is ever calculated.

Air is cross-ventilation as a layout decision. Two open sides beat one fancy façade, every time.

Community is our respect for how Indian families live with their neighbourhoods — applied through shared spaces, local connection and belonging, never as a slogan priced into a brochure.

Sustainability is lower bills and a lighter footprint: rainwater harvesting, solar-common areas, native planting and low-VOC finishes as standard.

One alone is a feature. Five together are a home. That is the whole idea of Tat:vm.

Light first, layout second

Blog · 4 min readRead →

How placing windows before walls changes the way a home feels — and what it costs to live in.

Most apartments are drawn as saleable area first and lived-in space second. Windows land wherever the façade allows. Flip the order and everything changes: the sun’s path, the breeze’s direction and the family’s day are drawn first — the walls come after.

In a light-first home, mornings begin bright without switching on a bulb. Afternoons stay gentle because the harshest sun never hits the main glazing. Electricity bills drop quietly, year after year.

It costs nothing at the design stage and is impossible to add later. That’s why light is the first fundamental — and why it will never be a premium option at Tat:vm.

Raahul Maroo on Mumbai’s next decade of housing

News · InterviewRead →

“The city doesn’t need more towers. It needs better homes in them.”

We asked our founder what the next ten years of Mumbai housing look like from a developer’s desk.

What will change most? — “Expectations. Families now ask about light hours, ventilation, running costs — not just carpet area. Developers who answer those questions first will lead the decade.”

And redevelopment? — “It is the city’s main course. The land is already there, communities are already there. Our job is to renew buildings without uprooting people — transparently, and on time.”

Why the five fundamentals? — “Because a home is a system. Light without space is a showcase; space without air is a box. We build the system, together.”
